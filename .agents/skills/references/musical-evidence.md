# Musical evidence and judgment

Use when interpreting track analysis, ranking songs or choosing transition regions. The AI chooses the strategy; PulseDeck provides observations and applies commands.

For track selection, phrase planning or a live handover, also read [DJ musicianship](dj-musicianship.md). Apply its handover plan before choosing transport/mixer controls: protected payoff, A exit, B entry/arrival, counted lead, bar phase, tempo/key strategy and fallback. This reference explains what measurements mean; it does not turn measurements into a confirmed arrangement.

- Report measured observations separately from musical hypotheses. A sustained rise/drop in low-band RMS supports a change in bass presence; a peak/RMS reduction supports lower amplitude or possible silence. Mid/high balance and repeating patterns support changes in texture. They do not identify vocals, instrumentation or a named chorus/drop/break with certainty. Never claim to have listened when only JSON was examined.
- Compare ranges within a track on the shared full-band amplitude scale. A short peak spike is not sustained energy; check multiple neighboring bins. Low RMS can still carry a prominent bass transient. Normalization, window size, clipping, missing bands and overlapping-bin timestamps limit inference. Cross-track waveform loudness is not perceptual energy/mood, and metadata rarely contains those labels.
- BPM/key confidence are algorithm scores, not calibrated probabilities. Consider provenance, ambiguity and alternative candidates; a manual/tag value can have different evidence than an audio estimate. Half/double tempo errors change every phrase calculation. A tempo match can fail if grids drift or the passage lacks stable beats; prefer a simpler supported cut/fade or a conservative loop on a verified grid when evidence is weak.
- Full Beat This! `final0` inference is followed by a native DBN/HMM decoder selecting one whole-track meter of three, four, five or seven beats per bar over 55–300 BPM. It enforces temporal/bar continuity while retaining locally varying beat timestamps; it does not support arbitrary meter changes or detect musical phrases. Inspect provenance and `rhythm_error`. Failure/warning requires a manually validated grid before automated beatmixing; a numeric offset or retained estimate cannot stand in for successful detection. Activation scores are uncalibrated and do not prove correctness or rekordbox parity.
- Inspect the effective `beatgrid_uniform` flag. Explicit manual BPM correction switches to a constant-tempo grid across the whole source, including half/double BPM edits. Offset-only realignment preserves neural timestamp intervals when that mode is active; it does not switch an existing uniform override back to neural mode. Manual bar-origin edits retain detected meter, with four as the unknown-meter fallback. Original neural metadata remains provenance, so its counts/BPM can differ from the effective edited grid. Playback tempo matching adjusts rate and does not require a manual BPM/grid-mode change.
- A grid origin is a timing anchor. A model downbeat is a predicted bar start; a manual origin is an explicit edit. Neither establishes the song's first phrase or arrangement. Declare meter and phrase assumptions, using actual downbeat spacing when available instead of labeling every fourth beat a confirmed bar. For Q beats/bar, a B-bar overlap contains B*Q beats. `beats*60/effective BPM` is a constant-tempo estimate only. For variable grids, count the returned events and use their source timestamps divided by playback rate for interval duration. Effective BPM = base BPM*playback rate; percent = 100*(target BPM/base BPM-1). Phrase progression must count both tracks from their selected positions; aligned grid phases can still have mismatched phrase counts.
- 8/16/32 bars are candidate structures. Look for recurring changes at those intervals, compare neighboring alternatives and list uncertainty. Don't force every song onto one structure. Quantize a proposed timestamp only when the grid evidence supports it, preserving the raw observed region.
- Harmonic compatibility is a ranking hint. Same Camelot cell, adjacent numbers in the same A/B ring (wrapping 12↔1), or same number across A/B can be starting candidates; estimated key, pitch shifts, modulation, melody/vocals and blend duration can invalidate them. Missing/weak key evidence means unknown compatibility, not incompatible. Key lock may protect pitch during rate changes but does not guarantee good sound.
- Prefer a blend when both passages have plausible rhythmic stability and compatible density; reduce overlap or use a cleaner cut when competing phrases, bass or uncertain grids make a long blend risky. Exchange bass deliberately rather than running two strong low ends at unity. Effects should solve an identified problem (e.g. bridge a sparse exit with a restrained tail) and leave known final settings; minimal effects is a valid strategy. Listening is needed to judge timbre, vocal clashes, perceived loudness, bass balance, phrase feel and artifacts.
- Set runtime is not the sum of track lengths if overlaps, entry trims, exit trims, loops or rate changes are planned. Provide a source-duration sum and a separate conditional estimate: sum of actual played windows adjusted by rate, minus overlapping wall-clock windows, plus loops. Avoid double-subtracting overlap/exit trims. Report actual session elapsed time separately; a nominal plan is not guaranteed duration.

## Phrase planning and launch verification

Plan backward from the musical handover: choose the outgoing section boundary and the incoming event that should arrive there (a main groove, vocal entry, breakdown, or another supported change). Select B's entry by counting the beats from that entry to its arrival event. Launch that many beats before A's handover. For example, at 120 BPM, B's entry 16 bars/64 beats before its main groove requires starting B 32 seconds before A's chosen exit. Do not default to B at 0:00 or A near the file end; silence, pickup notes, short edits and nonstandard intros change those choices. Identify both boundaries from listening, user-confirmed cues, or clearly labeled waveform hypotheses, and check what both arrangements do throughout the overlap.

Separate four checks: matching tempo, aligning beat phase, aligning bar starts, and aligning phrase/section progression. Equal BPM satisfies only the first. Sync aligns the effective beat clocks; inspect model downbeats or a validated manual origin for bar alignment, then arrangement evidence for phrasing. A beat-only estimate has no bar identity. A missing grid means unknown, including when the runtime exposes a numeric zero fallback. Model downbeats can be wrong; verify their transient/metrical consistency around the intended passage and farther into the track.

With credible same-meter downbeat origins, compare native beat positions modulo the bar length as well as modulo one beat. For example, A=349.476 and B=15.482 have nearly equal fractional beat phase but differ by about two beats modulo four: this is not an aligned 4/4 bar. The first decoded beat can be a pickup before bar one. Keep its offset in the entry-to-arrival calculation rather than treating the nearest beat to file zero as the intended musical one.

Keep the incoming channel closed while starting and checking alignment. Read each deck's native `beat_position` from the same transport snapshot and wrap B minus A to `[-0.5, 0.5)` beats. The renderer derives this value from the effective timestamp sequence; do not recompute it as `(position-offset)*BPM/60` on a neural grid. Convert phase to approximate wall time using the passage's local effective beat interval; `error_beats*60/effective_bpm` is a constant-tempo estimate. A roughly half-beat error at steady 123 BPM is about 244 ms. Use native queued launch, then recheck source revisions, playing state and phase before opening the fader. Repeat the check during an overlap because matching average BPM does not eliminate local drift. Unknown grids, null phase or unresolved analysis warnings require manual validation or an explicitly described simple fade/cut; do not claim a verified beatmix.

Use bars/beats for the planned transition, then calculate seconds. At 123 BPM, 8 bars are about 15.61 seconds and 16 bars about 31.22 seconds. An arbitrary 25-second fade is not automatically a phrase. Report actual timing separately, and skip a missed boundary rather than firing a stale launch after a model/tool round trip. Native timing fixes command latency; musical boundary selection still needs evidence.

Tutorials informing this guidance: [Crossfader's beats, bars, phrases and first-mix lesson](https://wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/how-to-dj-with-a-laptop-for-free/#mixing-in-phrase) and [Digital DJ Tips' phrase beatmix and bass-exchange transitions](https://www.digitaldjtips.com/rock-the-dancefloor/five-basic-dj-transitions/). These teach launch on a musical one, align arrangement changes, and exchange the low end deliberately; they do not validate the sections of an unlistened track.

Broad-genre Spotify checks exposed high model scores alongside octave switches and unsupported odd meter. Treat `bpm_ambiguous:true` in neural analysis as a passage-review requirement: the local timing may change substantially or switch half/double interpretations. Inspect the planned overlap's actual events and local pulse before automation. A regular model bar-gap count is imposed by the decoder and does not independently verify meter; changing-meter music is outside the default whole-track 3/4/5/7 bar decoder; candidate support alone does not guarantee correct odd-meter detection. Do not label such a case reliable merely because its events are finite and sorted.

Spotify can resolve a selected release to a provider-listed playable release. `status.source_jobs[deck].resolved_uri` records this when present; selected-source guards retain the originally requested URI. The app refreshes timing/envelopes for a relinked release and preserves explicit manual metadata. Verify loaded title/artist/duration and the actual timeline; do not bypass an unrelated-source rejection or change account/availability restrictions.

For a selected overlap, use `beat_events.local_tempo` from bounded `track.inspect` windows rather than treating the whole-track median as the passage tempo. It reports source BPM, p05/p95 and review status; native deck playback_rate supplies speed separately. A relative half/double pulse, a broad spread or reported acoustic pulse reconstruction needs passage-level validation. The detector's added midpoint events are declared in `neural_rhythm.bar_evidence[].pulse_repairs`; finite timestamps do not independently verify them.

Native Sync now matches local grid intervals, preserves a paused incoming cue, and maintains that match during playback. Enable incoming sync while paused, verify the cue/source remains unchanged, then queue the native launch against the master clock. Synced pending launches hold their prepared rate until firing. Explicit manual tempo/grid changes still cancel the launch. Whole-song BPM alone is not a reliable start-rate match for a changing or misinterpreted pulse.


Guarded half-pulse repair can preserve the dominant pulse through sparse passages.
Its `bar_evidence.pulse_repairs` records reconstructed interval counts, source ranges,
slow/doubled bar recurrence and flanking-pulse support. This is heuristic evidence.
`bpm_ambiguous` remains true after a correction even when local BPM looks stable;
`local_tempo.inferred_events_in_range` and `pulse_review_required` identify affected
inspection windows. Do not treat midpoint interpolation as an acoustic detection
or use a corrected bar grid as proof of phrase placement.

Bar recurrence scores/windows describe the independent clock before pulse reconstruction (after any guarded phase replacement). They are not rescored confidence for the published, repaired grid.
