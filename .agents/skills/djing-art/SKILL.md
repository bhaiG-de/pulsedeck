# DJing — the art

Compose what the listener experiences across two records. Selection, placement and mixer movement form one musical decision. The books' principles are synthesized in [sources](references/sources.md); runtime syntax belongs to the other skills.

## Own the musical performance

When asked to DJ, keep choosing and arranging throughout the requested duration. Use native scheduling to execute your decisions. An application-owned queue/Neural Mix is a different requested mode: it chooses successors and windows without reading this skill. A fitted native recipe can execute an excellent mix; its rank or name does not supply the musical idea.

Keep a compact working note rather than narrating every check. Read the [waveform reasoning procedure](references/data-to-decisions.md) before planning an unfamiliar pair; it explains how measured structure becomes useful musical decisions even without section labels. The plugin connection reports the resolved `art`, `waveform_reasoning`, `library` and `control` file paths. Use those installed paths and resolve other references relative to the file linking them; do not reconstruct a personal skills directory.

## Compose the next passage

1. **Set a direction.** From the brief, choose the next few moments: establish a groove, develop it, open space, increase tension, deliver a return, or close. Keep a small palette and a branching next direction, not a BPM staircase. For the closing choice, fit the complete statement and exit to the actual remaining music and set time; arrange a continuation or supported extension if it ends too early. Choose an unplayed record from the requested collection for its contribution. [Selection and energy](references/selection-and-energy.md) covers repertoire, history and longer arcs.
2. **Decode A and B.** Overview their arrangements, then count the decision windows. Retain effective grid/meter/downbeat identity, manual-versus-neural tempo provenance, and exact event objects alongside the structure. Map recurring foundations, thinning, developing activity, impact returns and continuation from low/mid/high envelopes, peaks and effective beat events. Compare neighbors and repeated patterns at several scales. Infer functional states and confidence; a missing “drop” cue does not prevent identifying a strong return. Locate A's worthwhile statement and viable exits, and B's complete entries, target arrivals and following development. For an unfamiliar rhythmic pair, obtain a counted bounded map of each selected passage through its next development. Capture `track_inspect` MCP evidence and use the [offline passage condenser](scripts/passage_map.py), or reuse equivalent complete saved maps. Its counted waveform packet keeps the temporal course visible; a coarse whole-track average is not that packet.
3. **Make the pulse and harmony work as played.** Before committing the shared timeline, choose the playing pulse for each rhythmic coexistence interval and calculate source-relative rate changes using natural local tempos, inherited rate and Sync. Prefer character-preserving changes and search nearby-tempo repertoire before unnecessary large resets. Morse's roughly ±4% is an ordinary-beatmix starting guideline, not an inaudibility promise. Match local tempo, phase and bar identity separately from phrases; check sounding pitch and exposed tonal overlap. Use [beatmatching](references/beatmatching-and-groove.md) and [harmony](references/harmony-and-pitch.md) when those decisions arise. If both sources carry exposed rhythm, derive a common pulse and alignment, or an intentionally counted ratio relationship; leaving both at natural speed is a deliberate tempo-exchange construction only when the passage map shows how competing pulses are avoided. Quantized launch alone matches neither rate nor musical bars.
4. **Predict their combined course.** Put both passages on one counted timeline from first audibility through the next developed phrase after clearance. At each structural change, identify who supplies foundation, rhythmic body and foreground; where activity is withheld; how long the listener waits; and what returns. Compare a plausible alternative entry or offset when the first pair produces an unintended hole, clash or jump. Runway establishes feasibility, not artistic merit. [Transition design](references/transition-design.md) gives constructive patterns and [worked cases](references/worked-cases.md) includes real waveform counterexamples.
5. **Place events, then derive controls.** Decide B's desired arrival, work backward to its complete entry and A's future launch, and count internal changes. Separate B playback, first audibility, bass transfer, foreground transfer and A clearance. They may coincide or differ. Derive sparse fader/EQ/filter/loop gestures from those events, with an ending for each gesture. Author a supported native timeline that delivers this plan. Do not move the musical target merely to fit the first proposal. [Phrasing](references/phrasing-and-arrangement.md) and [mixing](references/mixing-and-blending.md) cover counts and sound roles.
6. **Check the scored sound, execute and learn causally.** Use [library](../pulsedeck-library/SKILL.md) for exact version-specific evidence and [control](../pulsedeck-control/SKILL.md) for preparation, one timing owner and recovery. Read the transition `forecast` returned by `performance_validate`: place its timed source contributions, control exposure, pulse progression and coverage against your intended course. For a continuity blend, a source's strong low return is useful only when the scored treatment exposes it before the other foundation withdraws. Change the entry, offset or control movement when the forecast contradicts the intended relationship; a deliberate reduction instead needs a counted waiting interval and reachable return. Inspect unresolved coverage rather than treating a feasible score as a musical pass. Commit the chosen score, then verify actual source progression and control receipts against the designed events and forecast. Incorporate listening feedback when available. Separate a successful scheduled handover, a structurally coherent arrangement and an audible judgment.

## A complete placement decision

Suppose A's supported foundation runs from effective beat 480 to its withdrawal at 544, with a complete subphrase at 512. B's complete entry at beat 32 leads to a strong return at 64 and a developed groove beyond it. To replace A's withdrawal, B needs 32 beat intervals: launch B32 at A512, not at A480. Reveal B's useful approach while A carries, transfer foundation at A544/B64, and clear A's finished foreground at the chosen following boundary. If B instead returns at 96, the lead doubles and the launch/entry or support must change. The same track score and technique name cannot decide both cases.

For each unfamiliar pair, retain a compact counted source/role map before arming. It can reuse existing evidence; it makes the musical relationship executable without reinterpreting a long waveform response. A private plan fits in a few lines:

```text
Purpose / A statement to protect:
Measured A and B changes (seconds + effective beats), provenance/confidence:
Shared timeline: entry → first audibility → transfers → clearance → next development:
Predicted foundation / activity / foreground before, during and after; why this offset:
Each source: natural/local → playing BPM and rate%; pulse/bar proof; phrase counts; harmony:
Execution route fitted to those events; reachable musical alternative:
```

Scale the detail to the decision. A clear cut can need less than a developed blend. Neither short mixes nor outro/drop pairs are inherently wrong; their preparation, impact and continuation must fit the brief. Smooth-blend requests deserve useful audible contributions from both tracks.

For custom linear counts, [checked phrase plans](references/checked-phrase-plans.md) checks arithmetic, not artistic quality. After a seek, loop, delay or failure, recount from actual advancing positions and choose a reachable future event. Preserve the original set endpoint through recovery. For style-specific approaches consult the [style guide](references/style-and-phrase-manual.md).
