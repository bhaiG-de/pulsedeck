# Mixing and blending: build the sound between songs

Owns what the audience hears during the overlap. Use [phrasing](phrasing-and-arrangement.md) to select its musical boundaries, [beatmatching](beatmatching-and-groove.md) to sustain the pulse and [transition design](transition-design.md) to choose its purpose. PulseDeck control owns the commands. This reference describes musical roles, not fixed knob positions.

## Hear the overlap as a small arrangement

A blend develops a relationship before the incoming song takes over. Decide what B adds while A is still worth hearing: extra rhythmic motion, a texture, an answer, a new bass groove or the next foreground idea. If there is no useful simultaneous contribution, find better passages or another successor for a smooth-set brief.

Running two transports is insufficient. B at a closed fader contributes nothing to the audience. At the other extreme, two complete masters at full level can compete for attention and headroom. Plan the listener’s path between those states.

Keep five moments separate:

| Moment | Musical question |
|---|---|
| Playback entry | Where must B start so its later material arrives coherently? |
| First audibility | What useful contribution can listeners hear from B now? |
| Bass transfer | When does B have a foundation able to replace A’s? |
| Foreground handover | Where does B’s principal idea become the focus? |
| Outgoing clearance | When has A finished its useful support, before it conflicts or dies away? |

Some moments can coincide. A bass swap can be crisp inside a gradual blend; that differs from an instantaneous exchange of whole songs. Avoid equating a crossfader midpoint with musical dominance.

## Assign roles through the entire window

At each important internal event, identify foreground, bass foundation, body and top rhythm. These are perceptual roles, not isolated stems. A vocal can share its frequency band with snare and synth; a bassline can contain audible upper harmonics.

| Phase | A’s useful job | B’s useful job | Check before proceeding |
|---|---|---|---|
| Approach | Preserve its complete statement and pulse | Prepare or offer restrained support | Stable timing; entry retains required pickup; no premature vocal |
| Introduction | Remain intelligible and carry weight | Become audibly useful without crowding | Useful B content actually exists; level has room |
| Development | Yield the roles B is ready to carry | Establish rhythm/body and a recognizable direction | Internal entrances, withdrawals and tonal interaction |
| Handover | Finish its foreground; retain only helpful support | Supply the intended arrival and weight | Replacement arrives as old material withdraws |
| Clearance | Leave coherently before repetition/conflict | Stand on its own | Complete ramps, natural dynamics and final tone |

Trace the moments between the endpoints. A may lose bass eight bars before its final outro ends; B may introduce a lead eight bars before the marker called `drop`. A correct launch and arrival do not resolve those internal changes.

## Preserve weight without stacking bass

Ordinarily choose one dominant low-end foundation. Introduce B’s upper rhythmic contribution with its low end restrained when both sources already contain strong bass. Transfer weight where B’s replacement is musically ready and A’s phrase can yield. The exchange can be gradual or decisive depending on the basslines and intended impact.

Do not lower A’s bass because a preset stage says to if B contains only hats. Conversely, if A loses weight naturally while B already has bass, keeping B’s lows closed until A’s EOF creates an avoidable hole. Inspect the actual band changes and what follows them; file duration cannot choose the exchange.

A smooth blend does not require every band to be exchanged in one order. Complementary material may need modest level balancing and very little EQ. Busy material may need selective subtraction. Over-attenuating both channels can empty the mix despite two advancing tracks.

## Give the foreground room

An important vocal, melody or motif needs space to be understood. Arrange one foreground at a time unless a known complementary combination is intended. Let A finish an unresolved line or musical answer. Reveal B where its phrase can start coherently, then clear A before its next conflicting entrance.

EQ changes broad tonal balance. It cannot guarantee removal of one singer or lead. When two important lines collide, change the passages, the handover or the pair. Unknown vocal placement stays unknown; a low-mid RMS bin cannot certify an instrumental intro. Under uncertainty, reduce exposed tonal overlap and prefer better-supported sparse passages without claiming they are vocal-free.

A buildup creates an expectation about what follows. Replacing it with an unrelated sparse entry can feel like an interrupted sentence. Either preserve its payoff or supply a deliberate, prepared replacement of appropriate musical force. An exact downbeat alone does not justify the replacement.

## Match level while preserving dynamics

Prepare a sensible incoming level for the passage that will carry the mix, allowing room for its later arrival. Watch channel and master peaks and any limiter activity during the overlap. A limiter preventing clipping does not establish balanced loudness. Do not automatically raise master gain, boost every EQ band or normalize away a deliberate breakdown.

Fader values are controls, not perceived-loudness measurements. Two half-open faders do not guarantee one full song’s level; their curve and the material matter. Compare density, weight and articulation as roles change. A thin result calls for finding which role disappeared, rather than indiscriminately adding gain.

[Mixxx’s gain-staging manual](https://manual.mixxx.org/2.5/en/chapters/djing_with_mixxx.html) explains headroom and the limits of clipping. Its meter colors describe Mixxx, not PulseDeck. [DJ TechTools’ EQ lesson](https://djtechtools.com/2012/03/11/eq-critical-dj-techniques-theory/) distinguishes level-balanced full-frequency mixing, EQ blends and bass swaps, and explains why instrument isolation is beyond ordinary EQ.

## A developed blend example

This is an invented teaching arrangement, not a recipe for the loaded songs. Both clocks use credible 4/4 origins and play at 124 BPM. A’s statement resolves before bar97; its following groove lasts to a breakdown at113. B has a complete drum entry at9, bass at17 and a hook at25. The tonal and foreground relationships have been checked.

Launch B9 at A97: the approach to B25 and A113 is 64 beat intervals. After useful isolated timing verification, introduce B’s supporting rhythm while A still supplies foreground and bass. Begin the musical change early enough that listeners hear a developing transition. At A105/B17, exchange bass as B’s groove arrives. Through the next eight bars, let A’s foreground resolve and make room for B. At A113/B25, B’s hook takes foreground; clear A before its breakdown undermines the desired drive.

If B’s entry instead contains a competing lyric, or its bass arrives after25, that arrangement needs revision even though the arithmetic remains valid. If A’s useful support ends before105, move the replacement or choose another window. Do not preserve a teaching timeline over contradictory evidence.

[Crossfader’s three house techniques](https://blog.wearecrossfader.co.uk/blog/3-ways-to-mix-house-music/) demonstrates how the same pair can produce a gradual blend, an impactful EQ exchange or deliberate layering. Its demonstration’s band order and bar lengths serve those recordings; they are not universal defaults. The role timeline above is an agent-policy synthesis.

## Judge the result in separate dimensions

Check timing (pulse, bar, arrival), arrangement (complete statements and useful overlaps), balance (foreground, bass and headroom), flow (the desired direction survives) and execution (the planned controls actually completed). State which were observed and which remain uncertain.

A phrase-check pass with zero audible overlap proves a cut’s coordinates. It does not satisfy a requested blend. An audible overlap with colliding vocals is also a failure. User reports of abruptness, missing payoff or thinness are evidence to revise the relevant musical decision.
