# Adapt the musical method to the material

Style suggests useful questions. The exact recording and brief decide the answers. These are practical adaptations of the books' placement and programming principles, not a catalog of artist techniques. Read [phrasing](phrasing-and-arrangement.md) when the actual count is uncertain.

| Material / purpose | Productive starting relationship | Inspect before committing |
|---|---|---|
| Deep house / patient groove | Incoming rhythm develops as the outgoing statement resolves; a purposeful bass transfer maintains weight | Whether the intro really has enough body, and when bass/leads return |
| Repetitive techno | Complementary layers create a gradual change in the combined groove | Attack density, low-end interaction, small arrangement changes and whether repetition stays interesting |
| Melodic / progressive / trance | Outgoing melody gives space to incoming harmony and eventual foreground | Chord/tuning fit, long approaches, the promised release and two simultaneous breakdowns |
| Vocal house / disco / song-led material | Instrumental support under a completed vocal thought, then a new voice | Pickups, responses, returning hooks, live timing and real lyric boundaries |
| Hip-hop / R&B / pop | A concise complete-statement exchange, meaningful wordplay, or a prepared intro | Dense foregrounds, intelligibility, pickup timing and whether a familiar hook had room to land |
| Drum and bass / jungle / broken beats | A complementary rhythmic approach or decisive bass/foreground exchange | Breakbeat patterns, fast/half-time pulse mapping and whether both drops can coexist |
| Open format / large tempo change | A resolved phrase opens a new rhythmic paragraph, or an atmospheric bridge carries the change | Strong B entrance, complete A exit, pitched tails and the intended new pulse |
| Café / background / warmup | Welcoming continuity, patient development and restrained level/gestures | Intrusive peaks, long empty joins, excessive repetition, and the actual role in the venue |

A smooth café set need not use identical long fades. A drum-and-bass set need not double-drop. A melodic set need not preserve every intro or obey a key-wheel route. A cut can be musical, and a long blend can be tedious. The audible purpose matters.

If a user names an artist, establish which quality they want: selection, pacing, atmosphere, performance gestures or a specific routine. An artist's reputation does not specify a verified phrase map. Use a direct performance/breakdown when a signature technique matters, and distinguish the instructor's account from your own listening.

Transfer relationships: “B's voice answers A after its refrain” or “B's developed groove replaces A as its bass withdraws.” Then inspect the new pair. Copying an old track's sixteen-bar countdown, knob values or cue coordinates is not transfer.
