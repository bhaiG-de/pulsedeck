# High-precision beat and downbeat model candidates

Investigated 14 October 2026. This is primary-source research and bounded static inspection, not a measured comparison on the user's music. No package was installed, proprietary model copied, audio exported, or inference run in this research pass.

**Implemented direction:** full FP32 **CPJKU Beat This! `final0`** through **`danigb/beat-this-rs` 1.1.0 / RTen**, followed by native BSD madmom-style DBN decoding for temporal/bar continuity. The original minimal decoder remains a benchmark comparison. Upstream weights are explicitly MIT; no vendor or madmom pretrained weights are used. Published scores below describe their stated configurations, not the new application policy, and do not prove rekordbox parity on extended electronic tracks.

## Model comparison

Published GTZAN scores below are percentages. They come from separate papers and annotations/training setups; they are not a controlled head-to-head benchmark.

| Candidate | Beat / downbeat behavior | Published GTZAN beat / downbeat F1 | Code and weights | Practical fit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Beat This! full** | Offline joint prediction; minimal peak decoding, optional DBN. | **89.1 ± 0.3 / 78.3 ± 0.4**; three seeds. | Authors explicitly license both code and published weights **MIT**. | Preferred baseline; about 20M parameters, ~78 MB checkpoint. Native Rust full FP32 ONNX ~83 MB. |
| Beat This! small | Same outputs/decoder, about 2M parameters. | 88.8 ± 0.2 / 77.2 ± 0.2. | Same explicit MIT statement. | Lower footprint; not selected when accuracy takes priority. |
| **BeatNet** | Causal CRNN with two-stage particle filtering; also offline DBN mode. | Online 75.44 / 46.49; offline+DBN 80.64 / 54.07 in its 2021 paper. | Repository **CC BY 4.0**; inspected README/LICENSE does not separately spell out a weights grant. Bundled weight files exist. | Useful for causal microphone/live-input analysis; less compelling for preanalyzing a full DJ track. |
| **Beat Transformer** | Demixed instrument spectrograms, dilated self-attention and DBN. | 88.5 / 71.4. | Repository **MIT**; inspected README/LICENSE does not explicitly discuss a separate checkpoint license. | Eight 37.2 MB fold checkpoints; demixing adds dependencies and preparation work. The Beat This! README also notes restricted/copyrighted training recordings. An [author response dated 12 October 2026](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/issues/18) approves commercial use from the authors' perspective while leaving training-recording rights questions unresolved. Preserve upstream attribution and that distinction; the README's explicit MIT grant covers the released weights.

Two newer directions were checked. [BeatNet+](https://github.com/mjhydri/BeatNet-Plus) bundles ~3.07 MB weights and targets causal rhythm analysis; no LICENSE file was found in its inspected current tree. Its [published paper](https://reference-global.com/article/10.5334/tismir.198?tab=article) reports 80.62/56.51 F1 and 20 ms algorithmic latency, with 0.08 real-time factor on its setup. [BeatFM's paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.09790v1) reports 89.1/79.6 for MusicFM and 89.5/76.7 for MERT variants. Its Beat This! comparison uses the reduced-data 75.5-downbeat-F1 model, not full `final0`'s 78.3. This bounded search did not establish an author-released, licensed BeatFM checkpoint suitable for shipping. Neither displaced the practical recommendation.

## What the Beat This! scores establish

The paper uses default MIR evaluation, including discarding the first five seconds; it acknowledges annotation errors, metrical ambiguity and weaker continuity despite strong F1. Full-model downbeat CMLt is **67.3**, increasing to **73.3** with DBN while downbeat F1 decreases to **77.4**. It reports cross-validation HJDB scores of **98.2/96.6**, but HJDB is training material for `final*`; those figures cannot establish unseen-DJ accuracy. `final*` and `small*` must be evaluated on genuinely unseen data or GTZAN. A bar decoder is distinct from a musical phrase/arrangement detector. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.21658v1), [model evaluation warning](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this#available-models).

The source intentionally encourages overconfident predictions. A sigmoid value must therefore not be labeled a calibrated probability that a grid is correct. Return provenance and observed rhythm consistency separately. This is especially relevant around vocal-only intros, breakdowns, pickup bars and syncopated basslines.

## Native Rust port: inspected provenance and limits

Inspected port commit: **`1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40`**. Upstream Python commit: **`b95c8ab0c58c2d9fcfd40508ae8dffbc05ac4f5c`**. The [port's MIT license](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/LICENSE) retains both author notices.

- [Export script](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/scripts/ckpt2onnx.py) constructs the actual upstream `BeatThis` class, loads checkpoint hyperparameters/state, and exports FP32 beat/downbeat logits using ONNX opset 17. The full artifact is declared to derive from `final0`; the bundled small model derives from `small1`.
- [Release metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/danigb/beat-this-rs/releases/tags/model-large) reports full asset **83,162,650 bytes**, SHA-256 **`5f810debe53459b559127fb55bbad40035bb47cc567b20e501670f968c770f02`**. This was metadata inspection, not a full-model download/hash verification. Pin and verify this digest when acquiring the asset.
- [Public API](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/src/lib.rs) provides `analyze_audio(&[f32], sample_rate)`, consuming `analyze_owned`, and streaming ingestion. It returns beat/downbeat seconds, raw logits and the mel tensor. The stream avoids retaining all decoded PCM; it still analyzes the accumulated spectrogram when finished. It is not a causal beat tracker.
- [Inference code](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/src/inference.rs) uses 1500-frame chunks, six-frame discarded borders and `keep_first` overlap behavior, matching the inspected [upstream inference source](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/blob/b95c8ab0c58c2d9fcfd40508ae8dffbc05ac4f5c/beat_this/inference.py).
- [Postprocessing](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/src/postprocessing.rs) uses 50 fps logits, seven-frame max-pool peaks above zero, adjacent-peak averaging, then downbeat snapping to beat events. Individual frames are **20 ms** apart; plateau averaging can yield fractional-frame times. This does not guarantee sample-accurate transient estimates.
- [Python parity test](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/tests/python_parity.rs) compares one swing MP3 to Python golden output at **±70 ms**, requiring full-model F1=1. Timing differences are printed but not asserted. This is useful limited evidence, not bit-identical preprocessing or precision below 70 ms. The port uses rubato resampling rather than Python soxr, and version 1.1 addresses accumulated long-input resampling drift. No parity test was executed here.

## Implemented temporal/bar decoding and measured comparison

The minimal decoder has no bar-spacing constraint: independently thresholded downbeat peaks can produce implausible labels despite high activation scores. PulseDeck now consumes the same Beat This! logits with a native DBN/HMM, selecting one whole-track hypothesis of **three or four beats per bar**, over **55–300 BPM**, at **50 fps**. It retains locally varying peak-corrected event timestamps. This policy does not support arbitrary meter changes or determine musical phrases.

The decoder source is [madmom-beats-port, pinned commit `b5052828507dfee6f5329a9f1a9852732ac38990`](https://github.com/creightonlinza/madmom-beats-port/blob/b5052828507dfee6f5329a9f1a9852732ac38990/rust/madmom_beats_port_core/src/dbn.rs), adapted as a decoder-only module with [BSD notices](../crates/audio-analysis/src/dbn/NOTICE). Its code is BSD-2-Clause; its separate NC model files are excluded. The [upstream Beat This! adapter](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/blob/b95c8ab0c58c2d9fcfd40508ae8dffbc05ac4f5c/beat_this/model/postprocessor.py) converts sigmoid beat/downbeat logits with epsilon `1e-5`, supplies ordinary-beat probability `max(pBeat-pDownbeat,epsilon/2)`, and passes downbeat probability directly to the DBN. The DBN consumes those supplied activations and requires no madmom neural weights. The native implementation omits the port's additional parabolic refinement to establish the reference-decoder baseline.

The implementation agent's [validation report](beat-detector-validation.md) records these raw-minimal → DBN beat/downbeat counts on three 120-second EDM excerpts: **252/106 → 252/63**, **361/91 → 350/88**, and **253/66 → 256/64**. The decoded downbeat gaps are consistently four beats in these cases. Estimated BPM is **125.984 / 174.863 / 128**, against GiantSteps tempo labels **127 / 174 / 129**. These excerpts have tempo labels, not independent beat/downbeat annotations; the results establish constrained consistency and tempo comparison, not correct musical bar one.

Expanding the default range from upstream 215 to 300 BPM leaves those EDM event sequences unchanged and avoids a swing half/double-tempo failure: the 215-BPM policy produced **75.949 BPM, 192/48** events, while the expanded policy produces **227.488 BPM, 577/145**. Same-activation Python madmom 0.16.1 with matching 55–300 configuration matches all four native output sequences within **10 microseconds**, attributed to float32 timestamp rounding. Decoder-only processing is about **0.23 seconds** for a 120-second excerpt on the recorded machine. Reference parity and enforced bar consistency do not establish independent accuracy or rekordbox parity.

## Effective grid modes after manual correction

Decoded neural timestamps are the default effective clock. Offset-only **Set bar 1 here** realigns that clock while preserving local intervals. An explicit manual BPM/half/double change selects a constant-tempo grid across the whole source, rather than stretching a finite neural event list. An existing uniform override remains uniform after offset-only correction. Manual bar-origin numbering uses four beats per bar.

Native `beatgrid_uniform` and bounded `track.inspect.beat_events.uniform` distinguish modes; loaded effective flags are authoritative. `metadata.neural_rhythm` retains original analysis provenance/counts even when effective events differ. Unloaded source inspection infers a uniform override from `bpm_source:manual`. Playback tempo/rate matching preserves grid mode. UI and CLI share grid mutations; the exact refresh command is **`pulsedeckctl deck B analyze`** (`deck.analyze`), and manual overrides survive reanalysis. Failed-analysis warnings require manual validation before automated beatmixing.

## Independently checked mel graph

The [current upstream preprocessing source](https://github.com/CPJKU/beat_this/blob/b95c8ab0c58c2d9fcfd40508ae8dffbc05ac4f5c/beat_this/preprocessing.py) specifies mono 22,050 Hz, FFT 1024, hop 441, 128 Slaney mel bins over **30–11,000 Hz**, amplitude rather than power (`power=1`), frame-length normalization and `log1p(1000*x)`. The paper's 10,000-Hz prose differs; implementation fidelity should follow the actual code/model.

A small read-only parser decoded [the port's mel ONNX](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/models/mel_spectrogram.onnx) directly in memory. Artifact: **270,742 bytes**, measured SHA-256 **`fdd59e65c515331308e4c8841edf99972deca646bdf6197744c2a5b7755e3de9`**, producer metadata `pytorch 2.3.1`.

Observed graph constants are reflect padding 512/512, hop 441, frame size 1024, spectral scale **0.03125 = 1/32**, magnitude square/sum/square-root and log scaling 1000. Its 1024-value periodic Hann window matches the analytic window to maximum absolute error **1.95e-7**. Its `[128,513]` filter matrix matches independently computed Slaney triangular filters from 30 to 11,000 Hz to **1.28e-5** maximum / **4.77e-8** mean absolute error; the 10,000-Hz alternative has maximum mismatch about **0.999**. This supports the frontend's intended mathematics. It does not verify runtime operator numerics, resampler delay or complete end-to-end output.

## Performance evidence

The port author's [M1 CPU measurements](https://github.com/danigb/beat-this-rs/blob/1ae768e78f1ad83b0ed3886241dc29ffde853c40/docs/tasks/013-runtimes/performance.md) report a 4.5-minute full-model track in **4.245 seconds total** with RTen, of which **3.775 seconds** is beat inference; ORT inference is **3.371 seconds**. Model loading is around 20 ms for RTen. These are three-file author measurements on an older runtime/setup, not measurements inside PulseDeck. RTen requires no ONNX Runtime dylib; optional ORT enables cross-runtime validation. The current README's M4 Pro streaming-memory measurements indicate roughly 620 MiB peak for a 60-minute input. No verified CoreML/MPS latency guarantee was found. Measure the actual Mac under simultaneous deck playback.

## Integration and benchmark plan

These are recommendations for PulseDeck, not claims of implemented behavior:

1. Keep the loaded full model in a dedicated analysis worker. Supply decoded mono float PCM at its real sample rate directly through the native library; avoid temporary WAV exports. Gate result publication on track identity, analysis generation and manual-override version, and keep allocations/inference outside the audio callback.
2. Retain the detected beat/downbeat vectors as primary data. For a verified stable electronic track, robustly estimate tempo and offset from many events, reject half/double ambiguity and report fit residuals. Do not force a variable-tempo track or missed-event sequence into an unconditional constant grid. Manual correction must remain authoritative.
3. If transient refinement is added, restrict it to small local windows around predicted events and measure before/after timing and wrong-transient rate; a loud snare or bass onset is not automatically a musical downbeat. Retain the original model output to diagnose failures.
4. Compare Python and Rust on the **same predecoded PCM**, with model/export/version hashes, avoiding codec differences. Test 22.05/44.1/48 kHz, nonzero lead-in, impulses/silence, short tracks, 30-second chunk seams and long-input drift. Compare mel/logits, event counts and median/p95/max timestamp errors; ±70-ms F1 alone is insufficient.
5. Build an unseen, independently annotated **80–100 track DJ corpus**, stratified across house/techno, disco/funk, trance, garage/breaks, drum and bass, melodic electronic and pop edits. Include full tracks with first beat/bar, intros, pickups, drops, breakdowns, outros and tempo changes. Save annotations and derived metrics; do not redistribute source audio.
6. Report beat/downbeat F1 at 70/20/10 ms, median/p95 signed and absolute timing errors, CMLt/AMLt, first-downbeat errors, half/double errors, longest correct run, end-of-track drift and the fraction needing manual correction. Include the first five seconds. [mir_eval](https://mir-eval.readthedocs.io/latest/api/beat.html) supplies standard beat metrics; stricter thresholds and no early trimming are deliberate additional DJ tests.
7. Compare independently annotated ground truth against the same tracks/settings in rekordbox and PulseDeck. Reference agreement is separate from correctness. Phrase-aligned transitions also need arrangement/phrase annotations and launch-clock tests; a beat/downbeat model alone cannot establish good mix-in/mix-out choices.
8. [GiantSteps Tempo](https://github.com/GiantSteps/giantsteps-tempo-dataset) provides electronic-music tempo annotations and is a useful additional tempo benchmark. It does not substitute for full-track beat/downbeat and phrase annotations. Confirm any evaluation audio's access terms before acquiring it.

Until that benchmark is run, describe the implementation as a stronger, model-based detector with manual correction—not as demonstrated rekordbox parity or better.
